## 1. Program comprehension: measurement, metrics, cognitive models

### Works found

| # | Work | Summary |
|---|------|---------|
| 1.1 | **Syntactic/Semantic Interactions in Programmer Behavior: A Model and Experimental Results** — B. Shneiderman, R. Mayer, *Int. J. of Computer & Information Sciences* 8(3):219–238, 1979 (DOI 10.1007/BF00977789) | Foundational two-store cognitive framework: language-dependent **syntactic** knowledge vs. language-independent **semantic** knowledge built in layers into a mental model. The empirical origin of the "structure vs. meaning" distinction SSRL uses. |
| 1.2 | **Empirical Studies of Programming Knowledge** — E. Soloway, K. Ehrlich, *IEEE TSE* SE-10(5):595–609, 1984 (DOI 10.1109/TSE.1984.5010283) | Showed expert programmers use **plans, goals, beacons, and rules of programming discourse** for top-down comprehension; deviations from preferred strategies produce more errors. Evidence that comprehension is hypothesis-driven, not just line-by-line. |
| 1.3 | **Stimulus Structures and Mental Representations in Expert Comprehension of Computer Programs** — N. Pennington, *Cognitive Psychology* 19(3):295–341, 1987 (DOI 10.1016/0010-0285(87)90007-7) | Bottom-up model: programmers first build a control-flow **program model**, then a domain-level **situation model**. Good comprehenders switch between both; poor ones stay locked in one. |
| 1.4 | **Towards a Theory of the Comprehension of Computer Programs** — R. Brooks, *Int. J. of Man-Machine Studies* 18(6):543–554, 1983 (DOI 10.1016/S0020-7373(83)80031-5) | Top-down hypothesis/mapping theory: programmers reconstruct domain knowledge and map it onto code, refining a hierarchy of hypotheses. Hypotheses can fail to bind — the "missing/ambiguous/conflicting evidence" cases SSRL's evidence model must handle. |
| 1.5 | **Cognitive Processes in Program Comprehension** — S. Letovsky, *J. of Systems and Software* 7(4):325–339, 1987 (DOI 10.1016/0164-1212(87)90032-x) | "Opportunistic" model: comprehenders swing between top-down and bottom-up cues via **inquiry episodes** (ask question → conjecture → search code/docs to verify). Empirical basis for treating comprehension as interrogative. |
| 1.6 | **Program Comprehension During Software Maintenance and Evolution** — A. von Mayrhauser, A.M. Vans, *IEEE Computer* 28(8), 1995 (DOI 10.1109/2.402076) | Survey of six comprehension models + their own **integrated metamodel** (top-down model + program model + situation model + knowledge base). Explicitly flags scalability of small-program experiments and validity/credibility weaknesses. |
| 1.7 | **Theories, Methods and Tools in Program Comprehension: Past, Present and Future** — M.-A. Storey, *IWPC 2005* (DOI 10.1109/WPC.2005.38) | Canonical review pairing cognitive theories with tool support; argues tools should *enhance preferred strategies* (e.g., flexible top-down/bottom-up browsing) rather than impose one. Still the reference framing for comprehension-tool design. |
| 1.8 | **Cognitive Design Elements to Support the Construction of a Mental Model During Software Exploration** — M.-A. Storey, F.D. Fracchia, H.A. Müller, *JSS* 44(1):171–185, 1999 (DOI 10.1016/S0164-1212(98)10055-9) | Derives design elements for exploration tools from cognitive theory. Testable checklist prior art for SSRL's "progressive zoom / graph" projections. |
| 1.9 | **The Role of Software Measures and Metrics in Studies of Program Comprehension** — K.S. Mathias, J.H. Cross, T.D. Hendrix, L.A. Barowski, *ACM SIGCSE 1999* (DOI 10.1145/306363.306381) | Argues comprehension experiments must control the *nature/shape* of the software studied; metrics (cyclomatic, LOC, design complexity) are *factors*, not predictors. Direct methodological warning for any SSRL comprehension experiment (RQ-1). |
| 1.10 | **Software Comprehension — A Review & Research Direction** — M.P. O'Brien, ITB/Dept. of Computer Science working paper, 2003 | Non-peer-reviewed synthesis; useful taxonomy of bottom-up (Pennington, Shneiderman & Mayer, Detienne), top-down (Brooks, Shaft), opportunistic (Letovsky), integrated (von Mayrhauser & Vans), and knowledge-based (Brooks) models. |
| 1.11 | **Expectation-based, Inference-based, and Bottom-up Software Comprehension** — O'Brien (with De Lucia et al.), *J. of Software Maintenance and Evolution* 16(3), 2004 (DOI 10.1002/smr.307) | Empirical study splitting "top-down" into *expectation-based* (pre-generated meaning hypotheses) vs. *inference-based* (meaning from clichéd implementations), distinct from pure bottom-up. |
| 1.12 | **A Comprehensive Review on Software Comprehension Models** — A. Fekete, Z. Porkoláb, *Annales Mathematicae et Informaticae* 2020 (DOI 10.33039/ami.2020.07.007) | Modern review of the same cognitive families (top-down, bottom-up, combined) plus a catalog of what editor features serve which cognitive model. Confirms the 1979–1995 canon still frames the field in 2020. |
| 1.13 | **Empirical Research on Program Comprehension** — D.A. Boehm-Davis, in *Encyclopedia of Software Engineering*, 2002 (DOI 10.1002/0471028959.sof103) | Encyclopedia overview of comprehension models, measurement instruments, and known problems with them (esp. reliance on time alone; guessing measures; small handled programs). |
| 1.14 | **Correlates of Programmer Efficacy and Their Link to Experience: A Combined EEG and Eye-Tracking Study** — N. Peitek et al., *ESEC/FSE 2022* | Controlled study (n=37, EEG+eye tracking): high-efficacy programmers read more targeted, lower cognitive load, fewer errors. Notably, *experience levels do not predict efficacy*; self-estimation does. Suggests comprehension-speed is measurable and orthogonal to seniority labels. |
| 1.15 | **What Drives the Reading Order of Programmers? An Eye-Movement Study** — N. Peitek, J. Siegmund, S. Apel, 2020 (PDF via U. Saarland) | Replication study: source-code linearity affects reading order more than experience; comprehension strategy (top-down/bottom-up) has minor effect. Methodological caution for any study that assumes a universal reading path. |
| 1.16 | **Evaluating the Code Comprehension of Novices with Eye Tracking** — J.A.S. da Costa, R. Gheyi, *SBES/CBSoft 2023* | Three controlled experiments (n=128, C/Python/Java): clarified code cuts comprehension time 38.6% and attempts 28%; Extract Method cuts time 70–78.8%; visual metrics reveal effects static code metrics miss. Modern template for comprehension measurement. |
| 1.17 | **Eyes on Code Smells: Analyzing Developers' Responses During Code Snippet Analysis** — V.J.B. Martins et al., *SBES 2024* (DOI 10.5753/sbes.2024.3431) | Eye-tracking + smell study: long methods and feature envy raise cognitive effort; data class lowers it. Confirms structure/quality of code measurably changes comprehension effort. |

**Main insight.** Comprehension research is *old, empirical, and methodologically fragile*. The field converged (by ~1995) on a dual/opportunistic cognitive model — structure (program model / syntactic) vs. meaning (situation model / semantic) — and has spent 30 years measuring the same effects with narrow experiments (small programs, students/novices). SSRL's "facts vs. hypotheses" split is essentially an *engineered restatement* of the program-model vs. situation-model / syntactic vs. semantic distinction. The hard lesson from this literature is that **comprehension was always measured with time-accuracy-cognitive-load**, not with any "understanding score," and that conclusions rarely scale beyond small programs (explicitly flagged by von Mayrhauser & Vans, 1995, and Mathias et al., 1999).

**Relevance to SSRL.**
- **Comprehension gap:** supported only in spirit. The literature shows comprehension is costly and measurable, and that structure (syntactic/program model) and meaning (semantic/situation model) are genuinely different cognitive objects — which undergirds SSRL's core epistemic split.
- **Facts vs. hypotheses:** strongly supported. Shneiderman & Mayer and Pennington empirically separated syntactic (verifiable) from semantic (constructed) knowledge. SSRL's fact/hypothesis taxonomy has real 1979 precedent.
- **Confidence/evidence:** partially challenged. Brooks (1983) shows hypotheses live-or-die by binding to code (evidence), but no classic work assigns scalar confidence — that's SSRL's novel (untested) addition.
- **Primary projection:** the literature gives a *procedural* hint, not an answer: Storey's tools work when they let users switch between top-down and bottom-up freely, and interactive Q&A (inquiry episodes à la Letovsky) is the empirically grounded mode of comprehension. That weakly favors H1 (Q&A) over H4 (graph-first) as the primary surface — but no direct comparison exists.
- **Threat:** if SSRL validates with its own comprehension experiment, reviewers will demand controls for program shape (Mathias et al. 1.9) and modern instruments (eye tracking/EEG), not just self-report.

## 2. Code Property Graphs (CPG), Joern, CodeQL — and their semantic limits

### Works found

| # | Work | Summary |
|---|------|---------|
| 2.1 | **Modeling and Discovering Vulnerabilities with Code Property Graphs** — F. Yamaguchi, N. Golde, D. Arp, K. Rieck, *IEEE S&P 2014*, pp. 590–604 (DOI 10.1109/SP.2014.44) | Introduced the CPG: merges AST + control-flow graph + (intra-procedural) data-flow/program-dependence information into one queryable property graph, with a traversal DSL (Joern). Received the **IEEE Test-of-Time Award (2024)**. Core claim: vulnerability classes expressible as graph traversals. |
| 2.2 | **Automatic Inference of Search Patterns for Taint-Style Vulnerabilities** — F. Yamaguchi et al., *IEEE S&P 2015* | Extends the CPG idea to learning taint-style search patterns automatically from code patterns — shows CPG labels/structures can be mined, not just hand-queried. |
| 2.3 | **Code Property Graph (Joern documentation / CPG specification)** — joern.io / docs.joern.io (ongoing) | Formal, language-agnostic CPG base schema; notes storage evolution (Neo4j/Gremlin → custom OverflowDB) driven by scalability limits, and "overlays" to represent code at different abstraction levels. Production web frontend for querying. |
| 2.4 | **LLVM meets Code Property Graphs** — A. Denisov, F. Yamaguchi, LLVM Project Blog, 2021-02 | Practitioner write-up: mapping LLVM IR to the CPG; lists concrete gaps (no phi-node concept, high-level detail lost at IR level, type identity issues). Good evidence that even a "unified" IR loses semantic high-level information. |
| 2.5 | **CodeQL documentation — path queries / dataflow API** — GitHub/CodeQL (official docs) | Relational, semantic-web-style querying (source/sink/flow paths) used for security analysis. Illustrates that modern code analysis has standardized on queryable graph/relational models — but as *analysis*, not *meaning*.
| 2.6 | **Code Property Graph (talk, 2019)** — F. Yamaguchi, fabianyamaguchi.com/files/2019-cdl.pdf | Author's own account of CPG layers and — crucially for SSRL — *where CPG stops*: most false positives come from **context/business-logic facts not deducible from code** ("this is an internal service only our admin uses", "an invariant 'due to $aliens' keeps this integer non-negative"). Semantic limits are explicitly acknowledged by the inventor. |
| 2.7 | **Understanding Code Property Graphs** — RORO blog post (0xRo), 2025-08 | Independent technical deconstruction: AST is syntax-blind to flow; CFG is path-sensitive but data-agnostic; PDG quality is hostage to pointer-analysis precision; even merged CPG has path-sensitivity caveats. Practical catalog of the semantic gaps. |
| 2.8 | **Code Property Graph — Wikipedia** | Secondary source confirming history (Göttingen 2013, Joern Project, multi-language frontends, Ghidra/x86 binaries). |
| 2.9 | **LLMxCPG (LLM + CPG tool-mediated access)** — USENIX Security 2025 **[unverified — seen only via a skill-references file, not the venue]** | Reported finding: LLMs hallucinate CPGQL when writing multi-hop traversals directly; tool-mediated CPG access (an MCP server) wins. If real, it is direct evidence that SSRL's "LLM as worker, not oracle" principle holds for code analysis surfaces. |

**Main insight.** The CPG community answered "how the software works" with extraordinary precision (syntax + flow + data dependencies, queryable), and the inventor himself concedes it cannot answer context/business-logic questions — put another way, **the CPG has zero semantics for intent, domain meaning, or business rules**. That is precisely the empty column in SSRL's positioning table ("AST/CFG/PDG: accurate, no meaning"). The graph's value is bounded by what can be derived mechanically; everything else is inference and must be labeled as such. This is independent confirmation that a structural-graph-only layer is not a semantic layer.

**Relevance to SSRL.**
- **Facts (deterministic):** CPG/CodeQL-class tools prove deterministic structural extraction at industrial scale is a *solved problem* — SSRL should reuse, not reinvent (aligns with roadmap D-4 parser strategy and the "complement not compete" stance vs. CodeQL).
- **Hypotheses (semantic):** Yamaguchi's own limit statement (2.6) is the clearest published admission that code alone cannot ground intent/business-rule claims — SSRL's "hypotheses never promoted to facts" is the correct epistemic discipline for exactly this gap.
- **Primary projection:** the CPG is essentially H4 (graph-first) made maximal; enabling navigating a syntax/flow graph is *not* comprehension (RORO's own examples require meaning). Weakens the "graph-first" challenger as the primary projection relative to Q&A/narrative surfaces.
- **Evidence model:** CodeQL/Joern give SSRL a ready source of *structural evidence* (edges, flows, call chains) to attach to hypotheses — pre-built grounding machinery.

## 3. Software architecture recovery: surveys, reflexion models, clustering, limits

### Works found

| # | Work | Summary |
|---|------|---------|
| 3.1 | **Software Reflexion Models: Bridging the Gap Between Design and Implementation** — G.C. Murphy, D. Notkin, K. Sullivan, *IEEE TSE* 27(4):364–380, 2001 (DOI 10.1109/32.917525; FSE 1995 original, DOI 10.1145/222124.222136) | The reflexion method: engineer supplies a **hypothesized architecture**, tool computes convergences/divergences/absences against the source model. Deliberately treats the human hypothesis as an input — architecture is compared, not discovered. |
| 3.2 | **Reengineering with Reflexion Models: A Case Study** — G.C. Murphy, D. Notkin, *IEEE Computer* 30(8):29–36, 1997 (DOI 10.1109/2.607045) | Microsoft/Excel (~1.2 MLOC C) case: engineer reached working familiarity with Excel structure via reflexion models in ~a day + 4 weeks iteration, vs. an estimated "up to two years" otherwise. Strong industrial anecdote for structure-first comprehension. |
| 3.3 | **Characterizing Real-Time Reflexion-Based Architecture Recovery: An In-Vivo Multi-Case Study** — N. Ali, J. Buckley, *EASE/WICSA 2012* (DOI 10.1145/2304696.2304702) | First in-depth characterization of reflexion-based recovery in two companies on live commercial systems: documents actual modeling/mapping practices and the inconsistencies found — evidence of the messy, human-in-the-loop reality. |
| 3.4 | **Equipping the Reflexion Method with Automated Clustering** — A. Christl, R. Koschke, M.-A. Storey, *WCRE 2005* (DOI 10.1109/WCRE.2005.17) | Semi-automates the reflexion mapping via clustering (HuGMe); >90% of automatic mapping decisions correct in case studies — but the *manual hypothesis* remains central. Notes clustering "often fails to find the components a human would find." |
| 3.5 | **Software Architecture Reconstruction: A Process-Oriented Taxonomy** — S. Ducasse, D. Pollet, *IEEE TSE* 35(4):573–591, 2009 (DOI 10.1109/TSE.2009.19) | State-of-the-art review/taxonomy of architecture recovery; central observation: architecture is **not explicitly represented in code**, and recovery needs views ("viewpoints") + process, not a single graph. |
| 3.6 | **A Comparative Analysis of Software Architecture Recovery Techniques** — J. Garcia, I. Ivkovic, N. Medvidović, *IEEE/ACM ASE 2013* (DOI 10.1109/ASE.2013.6693106) | **Key empirical result:** 8 hand-verified ground-truth architectures; compared 6 state-of-the-art recovery techniques. "Even the best of the lot has surprisingly low accuracy." Direct evidence of the ceiling on automatic architecture recovery. |
| 3.7 | **Hierarchical Clustering for Software Architecture Recovery** — O. Maqbool, H.A. Babri, *IEEE TSE* 33(11):759–780, 2007 (DOI 10.1109/TSE.2007.70732) | Systematic study of hierarchical clustering algorithms/features for module recovery; representative of the large "recovery-as-clustering" branch that peaks in accuracy well below human annotation. |
| 3.8 | **Bunch: a clustering tool for the recovery and maintenance of software system structures** — S. Mancoridis et al., *ICSM 1999* (DOI 10.1109/ICSM.1999.792498) | Classic automated module-clustering tool via search-based optimization (MQ quality measure). Showcased the fully-automated recovery dream and, implicitly, its limits. |
| 3.9 | **Evolution in Software Architecture Recovery Techniques — A Survey** — M. Zahid, Z. Mehmmod, I. Inayat, *ICET 2017* (DOI 10.1109/ICET.2017.8281704) | Recent survey: architecture erosion/drift as the driver; clustering + ML approaches dominate; confirms "recovery" is still an open problem with no canonical solution. |
| 3.10 | **A Systematic Mapping Study of Software Architecture Recovery** — IKÜ / UBMK 2016 conference (openaccess.iku.edu.tr) | Maps 60 of 250 candidate papers (since 2000): ML-based methods dominate, only ~3 model-centric approaches; authors demand more empirical evaluation. Shows the literature's bias toward algorithmic clustering over model-centric/human hypothesis work. |
| 3.11 | **Software Clustering: Unifying Syntactic and Semantic Features** — J. Misra et al., *WCRE 2012* (DOI 10.1109/WCRE.2012.21) | Attempts to fuse syntax and semantic (concept/terms) features for clustering; representative of the line of work that keeps failing to close the accuracy gap. |

**Main insight.** Two empirical facts anchor this field: (a) **automatic architecture recovery has a stubbornly low accuracy ceiling** (Garcia et al. 2013: even the best techniques are surprisingly inaccurate against hand-verified ground truth), and (b) **the only approach that demonstrably works in industry is human-hypothesis-driven comparison** (Murphy & Notkin reflexion models, including the Excel case). The field's conclusion after 30+ years is that *architecture is a stake-holder-dependent, view-dependent interpretation, not an extractable fact.* Pure clustering "often fails to find the components a human would find" (Christl et al.), and ML-by-another-name hasn't fixed it.

**Relevance to SSRL.**
- **Facts vs. hypotheses:** architecture/module grouping is empirically shown to be a **hypothesis**, not a fact — SSRL is *wrong to be tempted* to treat "Component/Service"/"module Y belongs to inventory" as confident structure, and right to keep intent/membership probabilistic. Garcia et al. 3.6 is direct license for the "hypotheses carry confidence, never promoted" rule.
- **Comprehension gap:** the Excel case (3.2) is the strongest existing evidence that a *derived structural map + guidance* compresses the "nobody knows the codebase" problem — supportive of the SSRL thesis, though with one expert in the loop.
- **Primary projection:** reflexion models are the closest prior art to an H2/H4 hybrid projection (hypothesized conceptual model, mapped onto source); the evidence says the value comes from *comparing hypotheses to code* (H2 narrative/zoom), not from raw graph navigation.
- **Confidence/evidence:** there is **no published calibration** for recovered-architecture confidence levels — SSRL's confidence+evidence machinery would be novel here, and RQ-3 (calibration) has no benchmark to reuse.

## 4. Software knowledge graphs and ontologies: prior attempts and why they stalled

### Works found

| # | Work | Summary |
|---|------|---------|
| 4.1 | **A Toolkit for Generating Code Knowledge Graphs (GraphGen4Code)** — I. Abdelaziz, J. Dolby, J.P. McCusker, K. Srinivas, *K-CAP 2021* (arXiv:2002.09440; companion *Graph4Code*, Semantic Web Journal 2020) | IBM's large-scale code KG: program-analysis-derived edges + docstrings + StackOverflow links, 2+ billion triples over 1.3M Python files. Demonstrates scale but **no evidence of comprehension impact** — the graph powers search/API usage, not understanding. |
| 4.2 | **Semantic Code Graph — An Information Model to Facilitate Software Comprehension** — (authors via arXiv 2310.02128), 2023 | Dependency-focused model + tool (scg-cli) claiming better comprehension outcomes than call graphs / class-collaboration networks on 11 OSS projects. Notably *cites the absence of a comparative empirical evaluation of code representation models* as a field gap. |
| 4.3 | **Ontology Classification for Semantic-Web-Based Software Engineering** — Y. Zhao, J. Dong, T. Peng, *IEEE TSC* 2(1), 2009 (DOI 10.1109/TSC.2009.20) | Classifies SE ontologies and their uses; representative of the 2006–2010 semantic-web wave for representing software artifacts. |
| 4.4 | **Applications of Ontologies in Software Engineering** — H.-J. Happel, S. Seedorf, *SWESE 2006* | Frequently-cited survey positioning ontologies for SE (reuse, interoperability, process knowledge) — the optimistic program that never achieved broad tool adoption. |
| 4.5 | **Knowledge Graphs for Software Engineering: A Systematic Review... and Emerging LLM Integration** — S. Ahmed et al., *GRJNST* 4(1), 2026 (DOI 10.53762/grjnst.04.01.31) | Systematic review of 60 papers (2016–2025): KGs used mainly for code understanding/bug detection (40%), API dev (25%), security (20%); construction via ontology + extraction + embeddings; despite >80% precision claims in controlled experiments, **scalability, updateability, and deployment remain unsolved**. |
| 4.6 | **A Review of Knowledge Graphs in Software Repository Mining** — K. Jeganathan (Karthick), *AIRCC CS&IT* 16(11), 2026 | 56 primary studies (2006–2025), three-phase trajectory (static OWL/RDF → property graphs → LLM-integrated KGs); open challenges: no standardized cross-tool ontologies, scalability, and **scarcity of controlled user studies**. |
| 4.7 | **Knowledge Management Ontologies in Software Engineering: A Systematic Literature Review** — E.N. Udo, I. Umoeka, *IJRSI* 13(6), 2026 (DOI 10.51244/ijrsi.2026.1306000362) | SLR on KM ontologies: benefits claimed (sharing, reuse, quality) but barriers constant — **ontology development complexity, scalability, integration, user adoption**. Explicitly flags the lack of industrial/longitudinal validation. |
| 4.8 | **Enabling Analysis and Reasoning on Software Systems through Knowledge Graph Representation** — (TU/e technical report, pure.tue.nl) | Two-level labeled-property-graph ontology (structure/detail + container/type) for Java; explicit design goals include handling *partial and evolving knowledge* and multi-source accumulation. Recent (2023–2024) attempt at exactly a "derived, layered" software KG. |
| 4.9 | **A Systematic Mapping Study of Source Code Representation for Deep Learning in Software Engineering** — P. Samoaa et al., *JSS*, 2023 | 103 papers (2014–2021): AST/token/graph representations each capture orthogonal views; findings include **lack of generalization across tasks and scarcity of industrial validation** — representations are rarely reused or transferred. |
| 4.10 | **Semantic Representation of Software: An Approach Based on OWL (2 DL) over the System Dependence Graph** — Springer chapter, 2024 (DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-30573-2_14) | Formal OWL ontology over an SDG with a deterministic construction pipeline (ROSE) — a rigorous, recent example of faithfully encoding *structural* semantics in logic, with the (acknowledged) cost of heavyweight tooling. |
| 4.11 | **CodeKnowledge Graphs: Why Open-Source Stacks Stall at Enterprise Scale** — dev.to essay, 2026 **[grey literature, not peer-reviewed]** | Practitioner scorecard of Joern, Kythe, Glean, SCIP/LSIF, CodeQL, DIY Neo4j, agent repo maps across depth/coverage/polyglot scale/freshness/queryability. Argues most "semantic graphs" deliver *structural* depth, and staleness is a killer. |
| 4.12 | **CodeOntology** — (referenced inside GraphGen4Code/AIRCC review) **[[unverified] I did not open a primary source]** | RDF/OWL-Java ontology of code structure; the standing example of ontology-based code representation that did not gain mainstream adoption. |

**Main insight.** The pattern across 15+ years is consistent and slightly damning: **every "software knowledge graph / ontology of code" wave produced scale and precision claims but failed on the same three axes — maintenance (staleness), adoption friction, and lack of demonstrated comprehension value.** The 2026 reviews (4.5–4.7) say it plainly: deployment and updateability are still unsolved; user studies are almost nonexistent; cross-tool ontologies never standardized. GraphGen4Code is 2B triples and nobody reads it to understand a codebase. And 4.2 (Semantic Code Graph) openly states that *no comparative empirical evaluation of code representation models existed* before its own — i.e., the entire field predates calibration.

**Relevance to SSRL.**
- **Thesis confirmed (gap):** the failure mode of every prior "semantic layer" is exactly the one SSRL names as non-negotiable (P4/P5): staleness kills value, friction kills adoption. SSRL's *derivation, continuity, and dev-friendly* principles are the correct response to a documented graveyard.
- **Confidence/evidence:** prior KGs attached *no* confidence to ontology assertions — they presented semantics as facts. This is the single most defensible differentiation SSRL has: no prior work labeled hypotheses as such. The reviews (4.5, 4.6) calling for "better evidence" and "controlled user studies" are direct support for RQ-1/RQ-3 and the "comprehension is the metric" principle (P8).
- **Primary projection:** none of the prior KGs shipped a comprehension interface that beat the plaintext baseline; SSRL's projection research has no incumbent to beat — the field never resolved it.
- **Caution:** the ontology wave (2006–2010) died on schema rigidity and scale; SSRL must avoid re-litigating RDF/OWL and keep the schema minimal and derived (reuse the TU/e two-level lesson, 4.8, and Semantic Code Graph's "kept close to source with location links" lesson, 4.2).

## 5. RAG / GraphRAG for code: what they do, where grounding breaks

### Works found

| # | Work | Summary |
|---|------|---------|
| 5.1 | **From Local to Global: A Graph RAG Approach to Query-Focused Summarization** — D. Edge, H. Trinh, N. Cheng, et al. (Microsoft Research), arXiv:2404.16130, 2024; open-source at microsoft/graphrag | The GraphRAG paper: LLM-extracted entity KG + Leiden community summaries; answers *global* corpus questions where vector RAG fails; ~1M-token evaluation, comprehensiveness/diversity gains over vector baseline. **The authors explicitly acknowledge fabrication risk and limited evaluation domains.** |
| 5.2 | **microsoft/graphrag repository & docs (incl. DRIFT search, dynamic community selection)** — Microsoft Research, 2024–2026 | Note the current status line: **"largely in maintenance mode... won't be accepting new PRs or implementing new features."** A research project that demonstrated the idea and then stopped — with heavy indexing cost warnings in its own README. |
| 5.3 | **GraphRAG: Improving global search via dynamic community selection** — Microsoft Research blog, 2024-11 | Optimization of global search: LLM-rates community reports (GPT-4o-mini) and prunes; ~77% token-cost reduction with comparable quality. Shows GraphRAG is a token-cost story as much as a quality story. |
| 5.4 | **LLM Hallucinations in Practical Code Generation: Phenomena, Mechanism, and Mitigation** — Z. Zhang, Y. Wang, C. Wang, J. Chen, Z. Zheng, arXiv:2409.20550, 2024 (**also Topic 6**) | Repository-level hallucination taxonomic study (6 LLMs, CoderEval): three classes (task-requirement conflicts, factual-knowledge conflicts, **project-context conflicts**), the most-relevant-to-SSRL being repository/API/environment hallucination; a naive RAG mitigation gives consistent but *modest* gains. |
| 5.5 | **Citation-Grounded Code Comprehension: Preventing LLM Hallucination Through Hybrid Retrieval and Graph-Augmented Context** — J. Arafat, arXiv:2512.12117, 2025 | 30 Python repos, 180 queries: 92% citation accuracy with **zero hallucinations** using BM25+dense+Neo4j **import-graph expansion** and *mechanical citation verification* (interval arithmetic over retrieved chunks). The dominant factor was cross-file structural discovery, which pure text similarity misses. |
| 5.6 | **De-Hallucinator: Mitigating LLM Hallucinations in Code Generation Tasks via Iterative Grounding** — S. Halder, A. Kanade, P. Bhatotia (U. Munich), arXiv:2401.01701, 2024 | Grounds LLM output by iterative retrieval of *project-specific API references* into the prompt; cut edit distance 23–51%, fixed 63% of test failures caused by hallucinated APIs. Direct evidence that *grounding against the codebase* (not the wiki) is what fixes API hallucination. |
| 5.7 | **SelfCheckGPT** — P. Manakul, A. Liusie, M. Gales, 2023 **[referenced inside GraphRAG; not directly opened]** | Consistency-based hallucination detection in free-form answers (sampling-based self-consistency). Cited by GraphRAG's authors as the missing fabrication measurement — validating that even they treat fabrication as an open risk. |

**Main insight.** GraphRAG is a **fact-graph over natural-language text**, built by an LLM — so all its "facts" are *LLM hypotheses*. On code, the picture is sharper: the single biggest grounding win comes not from fancy retrieval but from **structural/cross-file information plus mechanical verification** (5.5), and the biggest hallucination class is *project-context/API hallucination* (5.4), which only the real codebase can fix (5.6). In other words: **retrieval over raw files under-grounds; structure over grounding with verification over-grounds.** The failure of vector-RAG on global questions (5.1) is an existence proof that "same-size retrieval" is not "same-size comprehension" — one of SSRL's core claims (related-work §8: "retrieval ≠ understanding").

**Relevance to SSRL.**
- **Confidence/evidence:** 5.5 provides an *evidential recipe* for SSRL: mechanical verification of claims against retrieved/extracted structure eliminates hallucination (zero in that paper). This is the empirical demonstration that "every claim links to code" is not just principled but *effective* (RQ-3 positive evidence).
- **Comprehension gap:** GraphRAG's own authors concede global/fabrication limits; the code-specific studies (5.4–5.6) prove generation-era tools still fail at repo-level context — supporting the SSRL premise that current AI "understanding" of repositories is shallow.
- **Primary projection (H1):** the strongest baseline SSRL must beat is exactly "LLM+retrieval+structure Q&A" (5.5); SSRL's differentiation is that the layer *persists, is derived non-LLM-first for facts, and labels hypotheses* — whereas 5.5's graph is on-the-fly greedy context.
- **Caution (anti-thesis):** GraphRAG in maintenance mode (5.2) and its heavy indexing cost are evidence that **LLM-extracted knowledge graphs are too expensive/fragile to be the durable layer** — SSRL's deterministic-facts-first design is the cheaper, more honest alternative, *but SSRL inherits the same indexing-cost problem* and must fail-fast on it (W4 probes).

## 6. LLMs for code comprehension: benchmarks, hallucination, repository understanding

### Works found

| # | Work | Summary |
|---|------|---------|
| 6.1 | **Evaluating Large Language Models Trained on Code (Codex/HumanEval)** — M. Chen, J. Tworek, H. Jun, et al. (OpenAI), arXiv:2107.03374, 2021 | Created HumanEval (164 hand-written Python problems) and pass@k. Codex 12B solved 28.8% (pass@1), 70.2–77.5% with sampling/test-filtering. Defined the *generation* centroid of LLM-code evaluation; **function-level, self-contained — not repository comprehension.** |
| 6.2 | **SWE-bench: Can Language Models Resolve Real-World GitHub Issues?** — C.E. Jimenez, J. Yang, A. Wettig, et al., *ICLR 2024* (arXiv:2310.06770) | 2,294 real issues/PRs across 12 Python repos; execution-based eval. Original best model (Claude 2, BM25 retrieval) resolved **1.96%**; even "oracle retrieval" 4.8%. Steep jump from HumanEval to multi-file reality; this is the benchmark family that keeps the "LLMs still can't do repository work" scoreboard honest. |
| 6.3 | **RepoBench: Benchmarking Repository-Level Code Auto-Completion Systems** — T. Liu et al., *ICLR 2024* (arXiv:2306.03091) | Three tasks (retrieval R / completion C / pipeline P), Python+Java. Findings: cross-file context improves completion, placement/order of retrieved snippets matters, retrieval is a bottleneck — the "give the model the repo, it still guesses wrong APIs" evidence. |
| 6.4 | **CrossCodeEval: A Diverse and Multilingual Benchmark for Cross-File Code Completion** — Y. Ding et al., *NeurIPS 2023 (Datasets & Benchmarks)* | 10k tasks / 1k repos / 4 languages built so that completion *requires* cross-file context. Even top models reach low EM (<20%); identifier/API retrieval is singled out as the weak point. |
| 6.5 | **On the Impacts of Contexts on Repository-Level Code Generation (RepoExec)** — (FSoft/VinAI), *NAACL 2025 Findings* | Introduces **Dependency Invocation Rate (DIR)** along with pass@k: code can pass tests while failing to use existing repo dependencies (duplication, reinvention). Full context helps; naive context formatting can mislead models. |
| 6.6 | **RepoReasoner: Evaluating Repository-Level Code Reasoning Ability of Long-Context LMs** — arXiv:2607.25996, 2026 | Output-prediction + call-chain benchmarks with oracle context: best model **69.1% Pass@1** on cross-file output prediction; high precision / **low recall** on multi-hop call chains; longer contexts don't consistently help (noise). |
| 6.7 | **RepoProbe: A Benchmark for Repository-Level Code Understanding** — Tencent-Hunyuan, *ASE 2026* (GitHub repo + protocol) | 500 open-ended questions from real GitHub Discussions over 50 repos, checklist-graded; 20 models. **Fluency outruns correctness** (e.g., GPT-5.2: 84.7 clarity vs 60.3 knowledge); best perfect-solve rate only **27.5%**. Architecture/business-logic questions remain hardest. |
| 6.8 | **CodeHalu: Investigating Code Hallucinations in LLMs via Execution-based Verification** — Y. Tian et al., *AAAI 2025* (DOI 10.1609/aaai.v39i24.34717) | Execution-verified taxonomy of code hallucination (mapping/naming/resource/logic); CodeHaluEval (8,883 samples, 17 LLMs) with a Hallucination Rate metric. Quantifies that syntactically-plausible-yet-wrong output is common and classifiable. |
| 6.9 | **Refusing the Impossible: A Taxonomy and Benchmark for Code Hallucination in LLMs** — V.A. Dasu, A. Kundu, G. Tan, arXiv:2609.03267, 2026 | Adversarial *unsatisfiable* tasks: across 12 open-weight models (4,332 judged responses), models emitted **ungrounded code on ~60% of unsatisfiable prompts** and refused only 27% (0% false refusals). Defines groundedness vs ordinary bugginess — separating "not grounded in the repo" from "wrong code." |
| 6.10 | **Detecting and Correcting Halucinations in LLM-Generated Code via Deterministic AST Analysis** — (FASE/Forge 2026), DOI 10.1145/3793655.3793725 | Deterministic post-generation AST + introspection-built KB catches **knowledge-conflicting hallucinations** (invented APIs/params) at 100% precision / 87.6% recall, auto-corrects 77%. Strong evidence that *static, deterministic machinery* is the reliable fix layer — LLM-in-the-loop repair is not. |
| 6.11 | **LLM Hallucinations in Practical Code Generation** — Z. Zhang, Y. Wang, C. Wang, J. Chen, Z. Zheng, arXiv:2409.20550, 2024 (**also Topic 5**) | Repository-level hallucination taxonomy (8 subcategories, 3 classes) across 6 LLMs; root causes include training-data quality and *repository-level context awareness*; RAG mitigation only modest. |

**Main insight.** The benchmark trajectory is a single story: **function-level generation largely works; repository-level comprehension does not.** HumanEval → CrossCodeEval/RepoBench → SWE-bench → RepoProbe/RepoReasoner is a ladder of rising context-dependency, and every rung shows LLMs degrading exactly where *cross-file structure and intent* are needed. The hallucination literature (6.8–6.10) adds that the failure is not random noise but *confident ungrounded generation* (60% on unsatisfiable tasks), and that **deterministic structural validation is the reliable corrective** (6.10, 5.5). RepoExec's DIR metric (6.5) and RepoProbe's "fluency outruns correctness" (6.7) are precisely the "generates code without understanding it" mode SSRL calls AI SLOP — now measured.

**Relevance to SSRL.**
- **Comprehension gap:** the gap is *empirically* real and currently measured by the community: a decade of benchmarks says models fail exactly on cross-file, architecture-level, "why"-level questions (6.6, 6.7). This is the strongest external support for the SSRL premise — not an intuition, a benchmark consensus.
- **Facts vs. hypotheses:** the hallucination work (6.8–6.10) validates the SSRL principle that **LLM output is always hypothesis, never fact** — and 6.10/5.5 show deterministic code-structure machinery should be the arbiter. This is RQ-3's strongest ammunition.
- **Confidence/evidence:** no current production model attaches confidence or evidence provenance to repo answers; RepoProbe's checklist grading (6.7) is a possible *evaluation instrument* for RQ-3 (does labeled confidence reduce over-trust), and unnecessary inventing of citations (5.5) is the failure to measure against.
- **Primary projection:** the "ask the agent" baseline (H1's competitor) is *measurably insufficient* today (6.2, 6.7), but improving fast — SSRL's value claim must be empirically tested soon (RQ-1), not assumed forever.

## 7. "AI slop" and software quality: serious evidence, if any

### Works found

| # | Work | Summary |
|---|------|---------|
| 7.1 | **"An Endless Stream of AI Slop": How Developers Discuss the Burden of AI-Assisted Software Development** — S. Baltes, U. Cheong, C. Treude, arXiv:2603.27249, 2026 | Qualitative study of 1,154 Reddit/HN posts: 15-code codebook across Review Friction / Quality Degradation / Forces-and-Consequences. Top theme is *structural drivers* (incentives rewarding volume, 26.2% of coded posts) — a management problem, not just code quality. Frames AI slop as a **tragedy of the commons**. |
| 7.2 | **AI Slop and the Software Commons** — S. Baltes, C. Treude, et al., arXiv:2604.16754, 2026 | Companion opinion/position: cheap generation + expensive review = commons depletion; proposes Ostrom-based interventions (review-readiness bars, provenance, walkthrough requirements, downstream-cost metrics instead of volume metrics). |
| 7.3 | **AI Adoption, Technical Debt, and Maintenance Burdens (Copilot DiD study)** — arXiv:2510.10165, 2025/2026 | Difference-in-differences across Microsoft-owned OSS projects around Copilot's 2021 release: productivity up (peripheral contributors +43.5% commits, +17.7% PRs) **but PR rework up 2.4%** (technical-debt signal) and — critically — **core contributors' original-code productivity dropped 19% while review load rose 6.5%**. Rigorous econometric version of "AI SLOP shifts the cost to reviewers." |
| 7.4 | **3100 Opinions on Code Review in an AI World: Building Causal Theory from Practitioner Discourse** — A. Agarwal, C. Miller, C. Kästner, B. Vasilescu, 2026 preprint **[unverified — found only via secondary blog, not a primary page]** | Claims agent-authored PRs less reviewed, merged faster, less discussed — but the authors reportedly state the *direction flips under other defensible analysis choices*. Useful as a caution about premature conclusions. |
| 7.5 | **AI Code in the Wild: Measuring Security Risks and Ecosystem Shifts of AI-Generated Code in Modern Software** — X. Wang et al., 2025 preprint **[unverified — found only via secondary blog]** | Detection pipeline over top-1,000 GitHub repos + 7,000 CVE-linked changes: AI code concentrates in glue/tests/refactoring/docs; certain CWE families over-represented; near-identical insecure templates recur across models (propagation via shared weights, "no version number/no advisory"). |
| 7.6 | **aislop CLI scan telemetry (264k scans)** — scanaislop.com, 2026 **[grey tooling telemetry, not peer-reviewed]** | 40.1% of repo scans find ≥1 slop-pattern; agent edits ~5× more likely to degrade a touched file than improve it (12.3% vs 2.5%). Not an academic paper, but the kind of instrumented data line W3 could tap. |

**Main insight.** Yes — serious work exists, more than one might expect. The strongest pieces are 7.1 (sociotechnical, hand-coded, reproducible codebook) and 7.3 (econometric DiD with a real control group). Their combined conclusion is **not** "AI code is bad" but a *cost-shifting theorem*: generation remains cheap, review/understanding remains expensive, and the burden lands on the expert core; compliance with meta-incentives (volume) beats compliance with quality. This is the systemic mechanism behind the SSRL "AI SLOP / AI DEV / DEV" trichotomy — and 7.3 quantifies the "DEV is a shrinking pool paying the comprehension cost" side.

**Relevance to SSRL.**
- **Comprehension gap:** 7.3 is the closest thing to a causal confirmation that **the gap between producers (SLOP/AI DEV) and comprensors (DEV) is real and widening** — peripheral producers up, core understanders down 19%, review up. If the goal is "make understanding cheaper for the DEV pool," SSRL is attacking the measured bottleneck.
- **Facts vs. hypotheses / trust:** 7.1's top user demand is *provenance and uncertainty surfacing* — reviewers want to know what was AI-made and how confident the generator was. That is literally SSRL's confidence+evidence design (O3, RQ-3). The discourse independently proposes the product SSRL proposes.
- **Primary projection:** review-time clarity (impacts, change-surfaces) is where practitioners feel it first; SSRL's "CI impact report" supporting projection has direct market demand (7.1's review-friction cluster).
- **Caution / conflict:** 7.3's effect sizes (2.4% rework, 6.5% review, 19% productivity) are *small and OSS-specific*; SSRL should not over-claim "AI ruins codebases." And 7.4 warns that several headline "agent PR" effects do not survive alternative analyses — W3 must treat discourse claims as hypotheses, not facts (a good SSRL self-application of its own principle).

## Top 10 most relevant works for SSRL

1. **Murphy, Notkin, Sullivan — Software Reflexion Models (FSE 1995 / TSE 2001).** Direct ancestor of "derived structural layer + human hypothesis, compared, iterated"; industrial proof the *mechanism* works. Maps to H2/H4 and the layer-vs-artifact distinction. *(§3.1)*
2. **Garcia, Ivkovic, Medvidović — Comparative Analysis of Software Architecture Recovery (ASE 2013).** The empirical ceiling on automatic "meaning from structure": even best recovery is surprisingly inaccurate. License for SSRL's facts-vs-hypotheses discipline. *(§3.6)*
3. **Jimenez et al. — SWE-bench (ICLR 2024).** The benchmark that made "LLMs can't do repository-level work" a touchstone; foundation for the comprehension-gap premise. *(§6.2)*
4. **Tencent-Hunyuan — RepoProbe (ASE 2026).** Nearest evaluation of the *exact* SSRL value: open-ended repo understanding; "fluency outruns correctness" is the AI-SLOP measurement; checklist protocol is a model for RQ-3 experiments. *(§6.7)*
5. **Arafat — Citation-Grounded Code Comprehension (arXiv 2512.12117, 2025).** 92% citation accuracy, zero hallucination, via structure + mechanical verification. The strongest single empirical case that SSRL's evidence-verification design is both feasible and effective, and the definition of the H1 baseline to beat. *(§5.5)*
6. **Shneiderman & Mayer — Syntactic/Semantic Interactions (1979).** Root of the fact/semantic separation SSRL operationalizes. Historical anchor for the whole epistemic model. *(§1.1)*
7. **von Mayrhauser & Vans — Program Comprehension During Maintenance (IEEE Computer, 1995).** The integrated comprehension framework + explicit validity worry about small-program experiments; the methodological conscience for SSRL Phase 9. *(§1.6)*
8. **Yamaguchi et al. — Code Property Graphs (IEEE S&P 2014, Test-of-Time 2024).** The definitive "structure made queryable" result — what facts the deterministic layer can rely on, and (with the author's 2019 talk, §2.6) where its semantics end. *(§2.1)*
9. **Baltes, Cheong, Treude — An Endless Stream of AI Slop (2026).** The serious qualitative evidence that the comprehension problem is now *collectively experienced*; practitioner-demanded provenance/uncertainty features are SSRL's spec. *(§7.1)*
10. **Dasu, Kundu, Tan — Refusing the Impossible: Code Hallucination (arXiv 2609.03267, 2026).** Cleanest operationalization of "ungrounded generation" (60% on unsatisfiable tasks); gives SSRL a measurable definition of the exact failure it exists to correct. *(§6.9)*
